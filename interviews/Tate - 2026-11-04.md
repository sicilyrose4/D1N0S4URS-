# Interview: Tate — 2026-11-04

- **Who:** Tate, a college student and outfielder from Lee's Summit, MO, who grew up on the lake. Tate was just cut from their school's baseball team and is deciding where to transfer. Tate also cut hair for a lot of people back home.
- **Where / how:** Phone call
- **Length:** 19 min (under the 30–60 min target)
- **Interviewer:** Quin
- **Recording / transcript:** Audio and AI transcript saved locally (not committed)

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** what's their situation, what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** what does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings… What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** who, how often, in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- **Who they are:** Tate just got cut from the baseball team and called it "a reality check" that's pushing them to work harder. They're talking to a few schools: one where players vouched for Tate because the team needs outfielders, and one in Kansas City that's closer to home but has a weaker baseball program. Tate feels stuck between the two. Baseball isn't "the biggest thing in the world to me anymore." What Tate mostly wants is a school they'll enjoy for years, a degree, and then a job.
- **Their days:** Morning classes, then the gym with their buddy Taryn at the same time every week (push/pull/legs split). After that, the same group of about 5 friends hangs out at Braxton's, Taryn's, or Tate's place: movies, video games, Walmart runs. In season, the team docks points for missed classes and hands out punishments like board pushes. Out of season, participation points and wanting good grades keep Tate going to class. Tate said "it's pretty easy to skip classes" in college because you're on your own.
- **Group activities:** See the table. On what makes groups work, Tate kept coming back to time together. Teammates you see every day "become your best friends," even ones you disliked at first. Tate said the friend group works because "nobody's really gonna judge."
- **Hometown friends:** Tate's childhood best friend (they "did everything together") has faded off. That friend is busy and doesn't text back much, but Tate is proud of them. Tate admits to being "not good over the phone," but visits feel "same as always" even after months without talking. Visits happen on weekends, sometimes at friends' colleges.
- **Cutting hair:** Tate called this one of the things they're "most proud of." It started after a bad haircut at Sports Clips, when Tate figured they could do better. Tate cut their own hair, then a kid's, and the kid posted it in the baseball group chat. After that everyone started coming in. It grew mostly by word of mouth, with a little social media. It built connections "with literally all ages." Tate thinks clients liked them because Tate talks first. The hardest part was time management around sports and school. Tate lost the clients after leaving home.
- **How they want to connect:** Casually, "just chillin," by going up to people and talking about anything. Tate meets people through mutual friends. Their favorite setting is a small, "chiller environment" with "my boys." New places are cool "but sometimes it's kind of scary too."
- **What's in the way:** Tate's first answer was "probably just confidence." They then started a comparison to talking to girls and asked to restart. Asked again, Tate said nothing is really in the way. The real worry is that a new school means "losing a lot more relationships than I'm building." Tate keeps in touch with close friends and lets casual ones go.

## Takeaways (LLM-assisted, then checked by us)

- **Time together builds closeness more than anything else.** Repeated time in a shared routine (team, gym, same houses) made people close, even people Tate didn't like at first. Tate didn't name anything beyond that.
- **Someone has to talk first.** Tate sees themself as the one who starts conversations. As a customer, Tate stayed quiet until the barber spoke. Groups may need a person or a prompt that breaks the silence.
- **Communities grow from a shared activity plus word of mouth.** The haircut "community" spread through one post in a group chat and then referrals. It was built around a real service, not around socializing.
- **Remote contact feels weak, but in-person contact picks right back up.** Tate doesn't keep up by text or phone, but friendships survive months of silence when they visit.
- **The biggest fear is losing relationships when life changes.** A transfer means losing the team, the friend group, and the routine. Tate sorts friends into "close" (worth keeping) and "casual" (let go).
- **Check this ourselves:** Tate named confidence as a barrier and then backed off. That could be worth asking about differently next time.

## What surprised us

- Getting cut changed how Tate thinks about baseball. It's now about enjoying school and getting a degree, not chasing the sport.
- Cutting hair was Tate's biggest source of community and pride, more than the team.
- Tate says they're "not good over the phone," and this interview was a phone call.
