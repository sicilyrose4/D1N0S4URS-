## What happened

- **Who they are:** Braedon is a busy student with a lot going on: neuroscience classes, a cancer research lab, music, the gym, philosophy books, and friends.
- **Their days (on a good day):** Wakes up around 7–7:30 and sleeps in running clothes with shoes by the door so they can go straight to the treadmill downstairs. Running "half asleep" is "a cheat code." Then a few minutes in the complex's new cold plunges, a shower, and eggs and spinach (Costco). Class at 9 (MWF) or 9:30 (TTh). On MWF there's a 2-hour gap, which is gym time. Second class runs 12–1, then about an hour in the lab, because their professor is also the lab director. Evenings are for friends or some Overwatch.
- **Making time:** Braedon asked their mom to set a screen-time limit of 45 min a day across all social media. They now use about 20 min. Without "TikTok and Instagram doom scrolling," Braedon says "sometimes I feel like there's too much time in a day." When that happens, they knock on neighbors' doors to play Wii or Super Smash Bros.
- **Group activities:** See the table. Braedon's main rule for making groups work: "there are no enemies." When there's conflict, ask yourself "how are they right?", even if you don't think you're wrong. Understand the other person first, then find the middle ground.
  - *Flag football example:* a teammate misses coverage and gives up a touchdown. Assume they were confused, not that they meant it. Conflict starts "once you start butting heads and saying, oh, he meant to be in the wrong."
  - *Lab example:* few people conflicts, because the professor hand-picks people who mesh. The friction is over scheduling and failed experiments. Braedon failed the same knockdown experiment for two weeks (each run takes about a week) and found it easy to get mad at themself or others. A grad student, Mike, told them "nobody's experiment works on the first try." One failure came from contaminating the cells under the hood. Braedon wrote it down in their notebook and learned to be more sterile.
- **Where the mindset comes from:** Philosophy and mindset videos on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram ("technology is humanity's biggest threat but also community's biggest ally"), plus listening to people who know better. Braedon also follows a **"five-minute rule": talk to a stranger every day for five minutes, then write down something learned from them.** Sometimes there's nothing to write, but the point is "the willingness to learn."
- **How they want to connect / advice for others:** Use the five-minute rule. "Most people want to talk to you... every human wants to meet another human." Just put yourself out there.
- **What's in the way:** Chasing other dopamine sources (social media and other habits) lowers Braedon's baseline motivation and makes them not want to go out and do things. Their fix is to cut out those habits.
- **Connecting over shared goals (the project question):** "In order to connect to other people, you need to connect to other people first." Braedon said you can't break habits alone and gave 12-step programs and religion as examples. Setting limits alone never worked; they'd always hit "ask for more time." It only worked once they asked someone else, their mom, to hold the password, because asking her to remove it would be "so embarrassing." Opening up to others makes the problems easier.

## Takeaways (LLM-assisted, then checked by us)

- **Small rules and setups make habits stick.** Braedon sleeps in running clothes, follows the five-minute rule, and has a mom-held screen-time password. Each one removes a decision in the moment.
- **Being accountable to someone else beats willpower.** Self-set limits failed; a limit someone else controls worked because undoing it would be embarrassing. People might connect around shared goals by holding each other accountable.
- **Free time from less scrolling turned into social time.** With less social media, Braedon "knocks on neighbors' doors." Being physically close (same apartment complex) makes that easy.
- **Group fit can be designed.** The lab works because the professor picks people who mesh. Flag football works because they keep playing together season after season.
- **Assuming good intent is how Braedon handles conflict.** Asking "how are they right?" keeps small mistakes from turning into fights.
- **Braedon sees tech as both the problem and the source of ideas.** They're open to tech that helps them connect but wary of the scrolling.
- **Check this ourselves:** Braedon is very outgoing and already has a system for meeting people, so they may not represent people who find connecting hard.

## What surprised us

- Braedon asked their mom to lock their social media, and they call that the only thing that worked.
- They talk to a stranger every day on purpose and keep notes on it.
- They say there's "too much time in a day" once scrolling is gone.
