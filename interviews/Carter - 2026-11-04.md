# Interview: Carter — 2026-11-04

- **Who:** Carter, a senior at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln in the social studies education program. Does his practicum at a middle school and works at a community center with K–5 kids.
- **Where / how:** Phone call
- **Length:** 40 minutes
- **Interviewer:** Sicily
- **Recording / transcript:** Audio and AI transcript saved locally

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** what's their situation, what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** what does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings… What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** who, how often, in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- He cares most about family, friends, and finishing his degree so he can "provide for myself and my future family." His biggest stress is time management: "there's not enough time in the day."
- Walked through his whole day (Election Day): up at 5:30, before-school program with 30–40 kids, practicum with his cooperating teacher and 40–50 students, lunch with roommates, gym, a drive to Omaha to vote and see family, a power outage at home, studying with a roommate, an exam, then the evening on the couch gaming with roommates. Pretty normal apart from the Omaha trip.
- He's around a lot of people all day (kids, crowds at the gym and student union) but interacts with very few of them. Real connection happens with roommates and family.
- Fridays are his only free day: hanging out with friends and chilling.
- Groups: his 26-person cohort, and club baseball, which he stepped back from because of time but still shows up to socially.
- Happy with the people he has and isn't looking to meet new people. Wishes he saw cohort friends and his parents more.
- Something that didn't happen: he tries to get his roommates to a weekly wings night, and they often bail ("too busy," "tired").
- Talked about how he works with others on shared slides, both as a student (co-planning with his cooperating teacher and partner, class group projects, whole-class Canva activities) and as a teacher (students posting answers to a shared slide, and the Chromebook tool that shows every student's screen).
- Brought up trust on his own: shared spaces, like a Minecraft Realm, need "a level of trust," and in-person relationships have consequences that online ones don't. That led into AI voice-clone scam calls.
- Talked about gaming and streaming: Jynxzi's mod controlling his PC remotely as a comedy bit, and live chat versus comments.
- Wondered whether tech is even needed: in his perfect classroom there would be no computers, but outside teaching he "selfishly" relies on it.
- Ended with his long-distance relationship: staying connected across distance is "really special and really cool."

## Takeaways

- **Time is the biggest thing in the way.** It cost him club baseball, limits cohort friends to twice a week, and keeps him from seeing his parents. Connection has to fit into routines he already has, like Friday gaming, lunch at home and studying together.
- **His days are full of people, but not much connection.** He's around 70–90 kids and crowds at the gym and student union every day, but his real conversations are with a small circle: roommates, a few cohort friends, family, his girlfriend.
- **He wants more of the people he already has, not new people.** Offline he isn't looking for new relationships, and online he avoids strangers and only wants shared spaces with people he trusts.
- **Trust is the gate.** Anonymity and strangers lead to trolling, stealing and scams. Even his example of trusting a stranger (Jynxzi's mod) is one hand-picked person.
- **He's the planner, but plans fall through.** Wings night depends on roommates who are "too busy" or "tired," and he can't predict when they'll be home. Not knowing who's around or free makes casual plans hard.
- **Distance is where tech earns its place.** His long-distance relationship, family in Omaha and an upcoming move are where he values staying connected digitally. Otherwise he'd rather do things in person, even "on a piece of paper."
- **When he works with others online, missing context gets in the way.** He can see that others are there, but not what they changed or why. That causes embarrassment (the surprise slide) and hesitation (not wanting to edit a partner's work).
- **Too many people at once breaks things.** At 5 people a shared slide is manageable; at 40 there are fights, deleted work and lag.
- **Low-stakes and temporary feels safer.** He'd join a live chat but not leave a permanent comment.
- **He stays in groups in lighter ways when he can't commit fully.** He stepped back from baseball but still helps with events and hangs out with the team.

## What surprised us

- How much of his day is spent around people without connecting with them. He's surrounded by people from 6:30 a.m. on, but his only real conversations were with roommates and family.
- He stepped back from club baseball but still shows up socially. Even when time forces him out of a group, he finds a lighter way to stay in it.
- He doesn't really know when his roommates will be home, even though they live together.
- He's skeptical of tech for group work even though he uses it constantly: "no computers in my classroom."
- Teachers can see and lock every student's screen in real time. That's an extreme version of being able to see who's there, used for control rather than connection.
