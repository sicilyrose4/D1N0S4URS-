# Interview: Evan — 2026-11-04

- **Who:** Evan, a master's in accounting student at KU and a graduate teaching assistant (GTA) for a writing-focused class. Grew up in Lawrence, and her family lives nearby.
- **Where / how:** In person
- **Length:** 20 minutes
- **Interviewer:** Sicily
- **Recording / transcript:** Audio and AI transcript saved locally

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** what's their situation, what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** what does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings… What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** who, how often, in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- Spends most of her time with friends, grading papers for her students, and doing homework. Her GTA job is supposed to be 20 hours a week but swings from 5 to 40, depending on how many assignments are due.
- Looking forward to Thanksgiving and Christmas break, mainly to not be in school. She's from Lawrence and sees her family often, so that isn't the draw.
- Stressed about her tax class ("intimidating") and the CPA exam ahead.
- Her friends call her "the leader of the group." She has mixed feelings about it: sometimes she doesn't want to be in charge, but she gets annoyed when someone else takes over.
- Walked through her day (Election Day): woke up to the sunrise alarm clock her roommate recommended, worked on a group project in her auditing class with two friends, emailed students, went to yoga, ran two hours of writing-coach calls with eight students, then picked up her mom and sister for a "voting party." They voted, compared notes on the ballot in the car, caught up on gossip, got drinks and fries at a restaurant, and then crashed her mom's dinner with a family friend visiting from Germany. She spent the evening alone in her room doing homework, then watched the election results.
- Says that's a pretty normal weekday: school and work in the morning, friends or family in the afternoon. Weekends are less about school: parties, bars, eating and watching movies on the couch with roommates.
- Free time is mostly at night, after homework: walks, yoga, or watching a show with her roommates.
- Feels most connected hanging out with her roommates on the couch, "just chit chatting, having a good time."
- Groups: a group project in each of her three classes, her GTA team (six TAs and the professor), and her friend circle.
- Wishes she saw her 87-year-old grandpa more, but feels pulled between family and "doing college."
- Her favorite get-togethers have all her favorite people in one place and a shared activity: Friendsgiving (everyone bringing a dish with a story behind it), Halloween parties, beer Olympics with teams.
- The one thing she'd change: people being on their phones while hanging out.

## Takeaways

- **Shared experience builds connection.** She bonds most with people going through the same thing: the TAs grading the same papers, classmates working through the same case. The small moments in passing ("guess what the student emailed me") matter as much as the formal meetings.
- **Connection comes from doing something together.** Her best hangouts have a shared activity everyone takes part in, like bringing a dish to Friendsgiving or competing in teams at beer Olympics. The activity gives everyone something to talk about and share.
- **She's the leader, with mixed feelings about it.** She plans and takes charge, sometimes reluctantly, but doesn't want to give it up. Groups lean on one person to make things happen.
- **Scheduling gets in the way of groups.** The GTA team couldn't all meet at once and the meetings have faded. Her TA hours swing from 5 to 40 a week, which makes her free time unpredictable.
- **Family is close by, which brings both spontaneity and obligation.** Living near family makes spur-of-the-moment plans like the voting party easy, but she also feels pulled to see family when she'd rather be "doing college."
- **Phones pull people out of the moment.** Being together isn't enough if people are on their phones. Her one wish is for more attention during the time people spend together.
- **She needs time alone too.** After a full day with people she sometimes wants space, and it's hard to signal that at home.

## What surprised us

- How much of a social and collaborative field she says accounting is, against the stereotype of accountants working alone.
- The "voting party": she turned a chore into a group event, with shared commentary on the ballot in the car and a drink afterward.
- Her favorite hangouts are activities with structure (teams, dishes to bring) rather than just being in the same place.
- She's very social, yet her one complaint is about attention (phones), not about time or access.
