# Interview: Noura — 2026-11-04

- **Who:** Noura, 22, a senior business major (marketing). Muslim; grew up in Kansas City in a three-generation Muslim family. A self-described extrovert who paints custom sneakers and jackets, does embroidery and pottery, and works about 20 hours a week at an art supply store.
- **Where / how:** In person
- **Length:** About 35 minutes
- **Interviewer:** Saj
- **Recording / transcript:** Written transcript in the shared interviews doc

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** What's their situation, and what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** What does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** Clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings… What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** Who, how often, and in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** Time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- Opened by saying "You picked the right person." She talks to people everywhere, including a 60-year-old man she met at the DMV and still texts.
- Learned to sew as a kid under the table at her grandmother's sewing circle in the basement of their masjid. Now paints custom sneakers and jackets, embroiders, and makes pottery and candles.
- Walked through her day: Fajr around 6, shipping orders, classes, the business school atrium ("15 conversations" per walk), art supply store shifts, painting until 1 a.m. Never misses Friday Jumu'ah prayer and the lunch afterward.
- Talks to about 100 people a day. When asked how many become real friendships, she paused and said, "That's actually my whole problem."
- She regularly organizes gatherings but ends up talking to everyone "for ninety seconds each."
- In her business fraternity, she doesn't drink and is comfortable with that, but social events center on bars, so she "gets the first half of every night" and misses the part where people bond.
- Her favorite thing is Sunday Spiritual Club, which she started in her living room. It grew from 3 to about 10 people, and it's "the only place in my life where all my worlds are in one room." About 40 people have come; about 6 are regulars.
- Felt most connected making six quilts over three weeks with her grandmother and community members for a family who lost their home in a fire.
- A four-week shared mural project doubled Sunday Spiritual Club regulars from 6 to 12.
- Graduates in May with a job offer pending in Dallas, and worries about rebuilding everything from zero.

## Takeaways (LLM-assisted, then checked by us)

- **Extroverts get stuck at the second conversation.** She meets people constantly; turning one great conversation into a friendship is where it breaks down.
- **A shared project creates the reason to return.** The quilts and the four-week mural pulled people back week after week. Single-session activities (craft night, paint-and-sip) don't.
- **Hands-on activities lower the pressure.** Painting side by side lets people talk without staring at each other.
- **Alcohol-centered socializing cuts her out of the bonding.** She's comfortable not drinking, but the closeness at bars and after-parties happens after she leaves.
- **Faith shapes timing and comfort.** Friday afternoon events conflict with Jumu'ah; events need a place to pray and halal food so everyone fits.
- **She wants her worlds to mix.** Her business, community, and art friends live in separate boxes. Sunday Spiritual Club is the one place they overlap.
- **Groups that depend on one host are fragile.** Sunday Spiritual Club and other gatherings happen because of her, and she's graduating in May.
- **Mixed ages matter.** The women in the sewing circle and the people at Friday lunch are the ones who ask how she's actually doing.

## What surprised us

- One of the most outgoing people I talked to said the follow-up, not the first meeting, is her biggest challenge.
- Her closest connections come from a masjid basement sewing circle, not any student group.
- Her business friends see crafting as "her little art thing," but everyone who has tried Sunday Spiritual Club has liked it.
- Shared work (quilts for a family, a mural for the prayer room) built more lasting friendships than any social event.
