# Interview: Amara — 2026-11-04

- **Who:** Amara, 20, a junior studying biology on the pre-med track. Muslim. Quiet and self-described awkward with new people. Works as an emergency department scribe two nights a week.
- **Where / how:** In person
- **Length:** About 36 minutes
- **Interviewer:** Saj
- **Recording / transcript:** Written transcript in the shared interviews doc

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** What's their situation, and what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** What does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** Clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings. What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** Who, how often, and in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** Time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- Arrived with notes on her phone "in case I freeze." Describes herself as awkward: she rehearses phone calls and Chipotle orders, and emails professors instead of raising her hand.
- Says she's "the funny one" with her close friends, and that nobody in her classes would believe it.
- Walked through her day: Fajr before sunrise, flashcards in bed, biochem, the library's quiet floor, Dhuhr in the union's reflection room or an empty stairwell, lab, studying until late. Scribe shifts Tuesday and Saturday, 6 p.m. to 2 a.m.
- Is most comfortable with strangers at her ER job: "I'm not shy when I have a job."
- Her core group is "the caravan": four Muslim women she met through MSA (two of them literally met hiding in a hallway at a welcome event). They travel together: Chicago in a snowstorm, Colorado, Eid trips home, and they're saving for Istanbul.
- On trips everyone has a role. Hers is food and prayer: she plans every halal restaurant and prayer stop on the route.
- Felt most connected during Ramadan, breaking the fast with the caravan almost every night for a month. A friend brought a newcomer, Mariam, who became "basically the fifth member."
- Big MSA events overwhelm her; she prefers the small Thursday halaqa (study circle).
- Left a pre-health honor society mixer held at a bar after 20 minutes. She doesn't drink, the room got too loud, and she "felt like I wasn't built for the way pre-med wants you to connect."
- Isn't lonely now, but two caravan members graduate in May and she'll be on her own for medical school, so she wants a wider circle.
- The only way she's ever made a new friend here is through her friends; the caravan's "one new person per trip" rule works for her.

## Takeaways (LLM-assisted, then checked by us)

- **Roles make quiet people comfortable.** She's at ease in the ER and on trips because she knows her job. Unstructured "mingling" is where she shuts down.
- **Her close group is her strength and her comfort zone.** The caravan gives her deep belonging, but it also makes it easy not to try anything new, and it's about to shrink.
- **New friends come through trusted friends.** Every new friendship she's made came from someone in her group bringing a newcomer. A plus-one turns an impossible event into a possible one.
- **The first five minutes are the barrier.** Once she's past the start, she's fine. An introduction from someone else, or a shared task, gets her there.
- **Side-by-side beats face-to-face.** Car rides, cooking, and reading in silence let her settle in before talking.
- **Faith shapes which spaces feel open.** Alcohol-centered events, Friday afternoon scheduling, events that run through prayer times, and having to explain her hijab all raise the cost of showing up.
- **Pre-med networking doesn't fit her.** She worries medicine is full of mixers she isn't "built for."
- **Travel is her best connector.** Long drives, shared planning, and shared stories ("now there were wolves") build the closeness she values.

## What surprised us

- She isn't lonely. Her problem is the opposite of what we expected: a close group so comfortable she rarely needs anyone else, right before it breaks up.
- She met her best friend because they were both hiding from the same event.
- She's now become the person in the hallway welcoming a nervous freshman.
- A silent book club was the closest thing to her ideal format, but its optional social part was exactly the part she skipped.
