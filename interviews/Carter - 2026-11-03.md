- **Who:** Carter, a senior college student studying education.

## Questions to cover

1. **Who they are:** what's their situation, what do they care about?
2. **Their days:** what does a normal day or week look like?
3. **Group activities they're already part of:** clubs, teams, classes, games, group chats, religious or family gatherings… What makes those work (or not)?
4. **How they want to connect with people:** who, how often, in what kind of setting?
5. **What's getting in the way:** time, money, distance, energy, confidence, not knowing where to start…

## What happened

- Started casual: Carter's "a little stressed out, school-wise," end of semester.
- Most of the conversation was about tools he uses with other people: Google Slides with his co-teacher and partner, Canva in class, shared slides his students post answers to, and the Chromebook monitoring tool teachers use to watch every student's screen.
- Moved on to gaming and streaming: Jynxzi's mod controlling his PC remotely as a comedy bit, and Minecraft Realms where people can enter your world while you're offline.
- He brought up trust on his own: shared spaces need "a level of trust," and in-person relationships have consequences that online ones don't. That led into AI voice-clone scam calls.
- Carter turned it around and asked how Sicily uses Figma, which led to a good exchange about everyone working in their own copy and then comparing.
- Ended on whether tech is even needed: in his perfect classroom there would be no computers, but outside teaching he "selfishly" relies on it.
- **Not covered well:** what his normal day or week looks like (Q2). Questions 3–5 got good material, though mostly through the lens of software.

## Takeaways (LLM-assisted, then checked by us)

- **Who he is:** a future teacher who is already on both sides of group activities, as a member (group projects, co-teaching) and as the leader (running a classroom). He's careful online and values in-person connection most.
- **How he wants to connect:** in person when possible; online with people he already knows and trusts; in low-pressure ways (he'd chat before he'd comment because chat feels temporary).
- **Trust is the gate.** He only wants shared spaces with people he knows. Anonymity and strangers lead to stealing, trolling and scams. Even his example of trusting a stranger (Jynxzi's mod) is one hand-picked person.
- **What gets in the way is missing context, not missing tools.** He can see that others are there, but not what they changed or why. That causes embarrassment (the surprise slide) and hesitation (not wanting to edit a partner's work). His one feature wish was a step-by-step rundown of edits he can accept or reject.
- **Too many people at once breaks things.** At 5 people a shared slide is manageable; at 40 there are fights, deleted work and lag.
- **The activity matters more than the tool.** He'd happily do it on paper. Tech is worth it mainly for distance and convenience.
- **Low-stakes and temporary feels safer.** He'd join a live chat but not leave a permanent comment.

## What surprised us

- He's skeptical of tech for group work even though he uses it constantly: "no computers in my classroom."
- Teachers can see and lock every student's screen in real time. That's an extreme version of being able to see who's there, used for control rather than connection.
- Minecraft Realms show the downside of shared spaces: people can come in and take things while you're away.
