## The prototype I used to test can be found here: (https://claude.ai/artifact/AVyWuf59e2UNpBrXfcjGJt)
I tested only the creation of the alarm and the action of turning it off and seeing the actions of the other users.

It starts with prompting the user to input their name, simply so they would recognize each other in the live test (this would not be used in the real app).

The user who typed their name first was prompted to create the alarm, and the others joined. Then, I prompted the creator to press a button to simulate 
the alarm going off at the time they set. I let them go through the experience, taking note of what they said out loud as well as actions they took. For the most part clicking the right buttons seemed easy, seeing as it is a fairly simple interaction.

I tested my family, so the example I used for the team was "Westside Warriors" (my brother's high school baseball team) so they could visualize this in a real-world context. It makes sense for them because my brother gets up everyday at 5 a.m. for team workouts.


## Notes

- They “felt stressed”
- “I felt pressured to wake up because if everyone else is awake and I’m not I feel left behind”
- “You don’t want to let your teammates down”
- “I don’t think they should be able to snooze” (because it feels like cheating)
- “I like the snooze because I might fall right back asleep if that’s not an option- I’m willing to do it even if it goes off for other people again (letting them know you snoozed)”
- “I don’t get the circles I don’t think”
    - But when I asked him to guess what they mean, he was correct. “What’s this outline around my icon.. oh that means it’s me right” and “so these are all the people?” He kind of just had to think about it to understand it, but he felt confused at first.
