# Multiplayer log: Nathan

Three multiplayer things I use regularly, and the design choices that tell me other people are there with me.

---

## 1. TikTok Live

- **Type:** Live video stream with chat
- **When and with whom:** I stream daily as the host. I streamed for about 8 months before, took a break, and started again recently. Viewers are mostly strangers, plus some regulars.
- **How long:** Usually 1 hour or more per stream

**Design choices that told me other people were there:**
- Live viewer count in the corner, going up and down in real time
- "[username] joined" messages scrolling at the bottom
- Comments streaming in, which I read and answer out loud
- Gifts and animations that take over the screen, so everyone sees who sent what
- Likes shown as floating hearts, which make the room feel active even when nobody is typing
- Leaderboards of top gifters, which give regulars status in front of everyone else

**How it felt / what I noticed:**
- It's two layers at once: me talking on camera, and the audience talking to each other in chat.
- Small signals like hearts and "joined" messages matter a lot. A room with 20 people and steady hearts feels more alive than a room with 50 silent people.
- Status mechanics (gifts, top fan badges) keep people coming back. People want to be seen by the host and by the rest of the room.

---

## 2. Figma

- **Type:** Shared design file
- **When and with whom:** Class projects and group work with classmates, plus my own projects
- **How long:** Ongoing, often an hour or more at a time

**Design choices that told me other people were there:**
- Live cursors with name tags, moving around the canvas in real time
- Colored avatars at the top showing who has the file open
- Clicking someone's avatar to follow their view
- Selection outlines in each person's color, so I can see what someone else is editing
- Comments pinned to specific spots on the canvas

**How it felt / what I noticed:**
- Seeing someone's cursor makes the file feel like a room, not a document.
- It can feel like being watched. I'm more careful when I know someone is looking.
- Cursor chat and following someone's view make it easy to point at things without a call.

---

## 3. Group chats (iMessage and GroupMe)

- **Type:** Group text chat
- **When and with whom:** Friends, class groups, and project teams
- **How long:** Ongoing, all day

**Design choices that told me other people were there:**
- Typing indicator ("..." bubbles) showing someone is about to say something
- Read receipts and "Read by" counts
- Reactions (likes, hearts, laughs) on individual messages
- Notifications and unread counts pulling me back in
- @mentions that call out one person in front of the whole group

**How it felt / what I noticed:**
- The typing indicator creates suspense, and sometimes pressure to wait for someone's answer.
- Read receipts create pressure to respond. If people saw it and nobody replied, everyone notices.
- Reactions let people show they're there without saying anything. A lot of the group is "present" only through reactions.
- Chats are mostly not live. People drift in and out, so it's hard to get everyone in the same moment.

---

## Patterns across all three

- **Small, cheap signals matter most.** Hearts, cursors, typing dots, and reactions show presence without asking people to do much.
- **Being seen is the motivator.** Gifts on TikTok, @mentions in chats, and cursors in Figma all work because other people can see what you did.
- **Live vs. drifting.** TikTok Live and Figma feel live because everyone is there at once. Group chats rarely do. Getting 5+ people in the same moment needs a reason to show up at a specific time.
- **Presence creates pressure.** It can be motivating (do the thing because people are watching) or stressful (read receipts, being watched in Figma). Design should decide which one it wants.
