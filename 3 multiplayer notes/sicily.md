# Multiplayer log: Sicily

## 1. Snapchat

- You can tell when people are there because their Bitmoji avatar is on screen
- You can see when they are typing, looking at an image in chat, or taking a photo based on what the Bitmoji is doing
- You can also see when they've opened a snap or chat because the delivered icon becomes stroked rather than filled
- So for Snapchat, the visual language is filled = unseen, unfilled = seen
- There is also the Snap Map, which allows you to see the location of all of your friends (who allow you to see it) on the map
- You can also see stories of your friends: purple stroke means you haven't seen it yet and no stroke means you've already seen it

**Takeaway:** Snapchat shows presence through a character (your friend's Bitmoji) rather than a plain dot, so you can tell what someone is doing (typing, looking, taking a photo) at a glance. One consistent visual rule (filled = unseen, outlined = seen) is used across snaps, chats and stories, which makes the signals easy to learn. The Snap Map shows presence in physical space, but only for friends who choose to share.

---

## 2. Find My iPhone

- Tells you people are there by putting their contact photo onto a certain place on the map, showing where they are physically
- There is also a list at the bottom that allows you to scroll through and click to find locations of certain people rather than having to search for them on the map

**Takeaway:** Find My shows presence purely as location, using contact photos pinned on a map. It's built for checking on specific people rather than browsing, which is why the list at the bottom matters more than the map. It's for people you already trust, and both sides have to agree to share.

---

## 3. Milanote

- Cursor indicator (like Figma) that shows exactly where people are on the canvas
- Also shows when they are present in a moodboard by having an indicator dot in the top right
- Also shows how many people have access to the document in general by showing avatar circles in the top right. This is different from Google because it's always present, whereas Google only shows them when those users are actively in the document

**Takeaway:** Milanote shows presence at three levels: exact position (cursors), being in the same board (the indicator dot), and access (avatars that are always visible). Showing who has access even when they're offline is different from Google, which only shows people who are currently in the document. It reminds you who's part of the group, not just who's online right now.
